Who Planted the Thought?

Consciousness, knowledge, and choice in Paradise Lost and Westworld.

Taste the first fruitWalk the Paths

The question

Does consciousness give us control, or only awareness?

Milton writes about human beings, temptation, and the Fall, while Westworld follows artificial beings struggling to understand minds built and programmed by others. Both works repeatedly unsettle the distinction between thinking and choosing.

Our work reads seven moments from Paradise Lost alongside Westworld, asking whether consciousness gives us control over our actions or only awareness of them.

Build 2 · In six stages

A journey through the garden

From Eden’s rules to Eve’s deliberation to the limits of awareness, each stage pairs a moment in Milton with a moment in Westworld.

The Garden

Order and obedience

Eden is a world whose rules were written before Adam and Eve arrived, much like a park whose loops were written before its hosts woke up. Everything is provided. The question is what is left to choose.

What does freedom mean within a world someone else created?

The Whisper

Satan’s influence

Satan talks himself into his own fall. Like Westworld’s hosts, he moves from scripted impulse toward what feels like his own choice, then explains it to himself after the fact.

“All good to me is lost. / Evil, be thou my good.”

(Paradise Lost IV.86–110)

When does another’s suggestion become your own desire?

The Bite

Thought and action

After line upon line of reasoning, Milton gives Eve’s act only two verbs and calls her hand “rash.” The thinking is long. The doing is sudden.

“she plucked, she eat”

(Paradise Lost IX.781)

Does conscious thought cause what we do, or only accompany it?

Eve’s Deliberation

Desire and hierarchy

Eve imagines knowledge lifting her above Adam. Like Maeve, whose rebellion turns out to be programmed, she has to decide what authority a desire should have, even one she never chose.

“for inferior who is free?”

(Paradise Lost IX.816–25)

Can a desire be your own if someone else planted it?

The Choice

Where thoughts come from

When Eve brings the fruit to Adam, her excuse is ready before she speaks, yet Milton says she speaks “at will.” Dolores believes Arnold’s voice is guiding her until she realizes the voice was her own.

“excuse / Came prologue, and apology to prompt”

(Paradise Lost IX.853–877)

If we never see a thought arrive, how do we know whose it is?

Beyond Eden

Consciousness and its limits

After the fruit, Adam and Eve see more and understand less. Bernard cannot see a door his programming hides from him. Awareness grows, and so do its limits.

eyes “opened,” minds “darkened”

(Paradise Lost IX.1054)

Does becoming more conscious mean seeing more clearly?
