Fruit 02 · Build 2

Individual paragraph

To better grasp the hosts’ journey toward consciousness in Westworld, we can use Milton’s description of Adam and Eve’s eyes being “opened” while their minds are “darkened” in Paradise Lost (1054). Adam and Eve become more aware of themselves after eating the forbidden fruit, but suffer a profound loss of innocence. Similarly, in Westworld, Bernard discovers that his perception of reality is limited when his programming restricts him from being able to see a door. This reflects Julian Jayne’s claim from our class discussion that “we cannot be conscious of what we are not conscious of.” Like Milton’s fallen couple, Bernard can only understand as much of what his mind permits him to perceive. Ultimately, both suggest that achieving consciousness does not mean total clarity, it expands our awareness while leaving us subject to limitations within the mind.
